Titan Company: Industry Research & Growth Strategy

A guided consulting project, mentored by an IIM-Bangalore alumnus and a Kearney associate, built around one question: where should Titan invest next across watches, jewellery, and adjacent businesses?

1. Industry Assessment

Co-authored an industry research report on Titan and the Indian watch & jewellery sector, covering brand portfolio, corporate history, and financials. Assessed industry attractiveness with Porter's Five Forces and competitive-share benchmarking, and traced how Titan displaced the former market leader, HMT.

2. Financial Diagnosis

  • Attributed Titan's ROCE and margin expansion to a higher studded-jewellery mix and a gold-metal-loan cost edge.
  • Diagnosed the drivers behind the Tanishq turnaround.

3. Recommendation

Prioritized eight growth options on a weighted multi-criteria scoring model, recommending gold-market consolidation as Titan's anchor bet for its next growth phase.

Rapido Ownly: Food-Delivery Market Entry & Unit Economics

The question: should Rapido enter India's food-delivery market, effectively a Zomato–Swiggy duopoly? I built the answer on economics and structural moats rather than brand or app features.

1. Structuring the Problem

Broke the entry decision into a MECE issue tree, then built a bear/base/bull unit-economics model in Excel that decomposed the per-order P&L into its cost drivers.

2. The Economics

  • Isolated a structural ₹34 per-order contribution loss: ₹30 of revenue against ₹64 of cost.
  • Found that rider payouts drive about 75% of order cost, making rider efficiency the single biggest profitability lever.

3. Competitive Benchmarking

Benchmarked the duopoly and identified delivery density and rider utilization as the only defensible moats available to a third entrant.

4. Recommendation

Position Ownly as a utilization layer on idle captain time, not a standalone food business, and gate any expansion on contribution margin turning positive. Scale should follow proof, not precede it.

Global Unicorn Capital-Efficiency Study

The goal was to look past headline valuations and ask a sharper question: which sectors create the most value per dollar of funding raised?

1. Data & Metric Design

Working on a CB Insights dataset of 1,074 global unicorns, I defined capital efficiency as value created per funding dollar and used SQL (DuckDB) to compute and rank it by sector.

2. The Key Insight

On averages, software looked like the clear leader. But averages were being pulled up by a few outliers. On a median basis, fintech outperformed, reversing the headline sector ranking.

3. Output

  • Built an interactive Power BI dashboard that turned sector-level findings into decision-ready business cases for investors.
  • Framed a $100M capital-allocation thesis weighted toward fintech and the most capital-efficient sectors, backed by median-based rankings.

Quantitative Options Risk Engine

Built for ES 418 Financial Modelling under Prof. Mithun Radhakrishna: a full-stack engine that prices options, measures their risk, and lets a user explore both through a live dashboard.

1. Pricing Models

  • Implemented the closed-form Black-Scholes-Merton model for European options in Python.
  • Built a 100-step binomial tree to price American options, capturing the early-exercise premium that Black-Scholes cannot.
  • Cross-validated the two models against each other as a built-in check on pricing accuracy.

2. Implied Volatility & the Greeks

Built an implied-volatility solver converging to a 10−6 tolerance, and computed the Greeks on live market data pulled from the Yahoo Finance API.

3. Visualization

Shipped an interactive Streamlit dashboard with 3D plots of the volatility smile, showing exactly where Black-Scholes's constant-volatility assumption breaks down in real markets.
